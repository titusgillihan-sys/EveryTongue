What we're checking, in plain English

In English, you can say a word high, low, rising, or flat, and it's still the same word — you've just changed your tone of voice. Mandarin Chinese doesn't work that way. The pitch you use on a syllable isn't tone of voice — it's part of the word itself. Say "ma" with a rising pitch and it means "hemp." Say it with a falling pitch and it means "scold." Same sound, different pitch, completely different word.

That's a problem for singing. A song's melody locks every syllable to a fixed note, whether or not that note matches the word's real pitch. So when a hymn gets translated into Mandarin but kept on its original Western tune, the melody can push a word's pitch the wrong way — and the lyric quietly turns into a different word than the translator meant.

This tool finds exactly where that happens. Below, a real hymn line is checked syllable by syllable against its melody. Where the tune and the tone disagree, it shows you the word that's supposed to be there, the word the melody accidentally creates instead, and a fix that keeps both the meaning and the tune.
